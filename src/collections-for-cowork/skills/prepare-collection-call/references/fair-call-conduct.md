# Fair call conduct rules for collection briefs

These rules shape every brief. They reflect fair, truthful and non-threatening collection practice for business accounts. They are not legal advice; where the debtor is an individual or sole proprietor, consumer-debt rules (in the US, for example, the FDCPA and state laws such as those that extend similar duties to original creditors) may impose further requirements, and the brief is routed to the user's compliance review.

## Never script

- A threat of any action that is not lawful, not permitted by the contract or policy, or not actually intended (for example "we will sue tomorrow" when no referral is approved).
- A false or exaggerated amount, including fees or interest the contract does not provide for.
- False urgency or invented deadlines ("today is the last chance") unless the date is a real policy step the user has confirmed.
- Statements implying the caller is a lawyer, an authority, or a third-party agency.
- Disclosure of the debt to anyone other than the customer's authorized contacts (no discussion with receptionists, suppliers or other customers beyond identifying the caller and asking for the contact).
- Insults, raised-pressure language, repeated calls intended to annoy, or calls outside permitted hours in the customer's local time.
- Demands for payment of amounts that are in dispute.
- Comments on the customer's financial health drawn from internal credit analysis.

## Always include

- The specific invoices, amounts and due dates being discussed, from the ledger.
- Acknowledgment of open disputes and their owner.
- A clear, dated ask and how to pay, using only the company's approved remittance details from the user's template or profile.
- A confirmation step: restate amount, date and method of any promise before ending the call.
- An exit: if the contact says they cannot discuss payment or asks for written communication only, the collector agrees and records it.

## Permitted factual statements about consequences

Only when true, provided for by the contract or the credit policy, and confirmed as intended by the user:

- "Orders that would take the account over its credit limit are reviewed by our credit team before release."
- "Under the agreement, late payment interest applies at the contractual rate" — only when the contract contains such a clause; cite it in the brief.
- "If we cannot agree a payment date, the account will be reviewed by our credit manager on <date>."

## Account-specific cautions to check

- Customer in insolvency, administration or a restructuring process: no collection call; refer to counsel.
- Customer has an open complaint or legal correspondence: involve the account manager or counsel as the policy requires.
- Contact preference recorded (email only, specific hours, named contact): honor it.
- Possible consumer debt: mark `Compliance review`; no call until reviewed.
